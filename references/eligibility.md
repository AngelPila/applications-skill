# Eligibility filtering

Do this before spending any time on a form. Quote the exact sentence from the official page for every decision.

## Hard excluders (skip and record the reason)

| Signal on the page | Meaning |
|---|---|
| "must be legally eligible/authorized to work in [country]" + "sponsorship is not available" | Excluded unless the applicant already has work rights there. |
| "must have the right to work in [country] for the full duration" | Same. |
| "currently enrolled in a [country] university/degree program" (as a requirement) | Excluded. If it is only "preferred", keep with risk. |
| "US citizens / permanent residents only", export-control clearance | Excluded for others. |
| Graduation window that the applicant's expected graduation does not fall into | Excluded (do not bend the date). |
| "penultimate year" when the applicant is not in it | Excluded. |
| Deadline passed | Move to "Upcoming" with next cycle's expected date. |
| PhD-only / Master's-only | Excluded for undergrads. |

## Risks (keep, but tell the applicant)

- Sponsorship not stated, but the form asks "will you require sponsorship?" → apply, answer honestly, expect screening.
- Minimum GPA on another scale (e.g. 3.6/4.0 "or equivalent") → note the applicant's real GPA and scale; do not convert upward.
- Official/certified translation required (vs. "unofficial accepted").
- Program dates clash with the applicant's academic calendar.
- Long or off-cycle internships (e.g. 6 months) that require a university internship agreement.
- Visa processing time (Schengen, UK, US J-1/F-1, Qatar, Singapore…) vs. start date.
- Limits: "one application per season", "only one role at a time", "one application every 30 days", "max N roles", cool-down after rejection (e.g. 8 months). Pick the single best role/location.
- Proctored assessments within N days of applying: schedule the application when the applicant has time to prepare.

## Phrases that usually mean "open to international students"

- "open to all students worldwide", "regardless of nationality"
- "we will support/assist with the visa process", "immigration sponsorship can be provided", "relocation package"
- "international students are eligible" (still check location-specific restrictions, e.g. lab sites limited to citizens)

## Typical families and what to check

| Family | Check |
|---|---|
| Big tech internships (US) | Usually require US enrollment or work authorization (CPT/OPT). EMEA/APAC offices may differ: check each posting. |
| Trading firms (London, Amsterdam, Singapore) | Often sponsor or relocate; strict graduation-year windows; one application per firm; fast online assessments. |
| Research summer programs (EU, Asia, Middle East) | Usually open worldwide; need transcript, motivation letter, sometimes references; deadlines Nov–Feb. |
| Remote fellowships / open source (MLH, LFX, Outreachy, GSoC) | Open worldwide (check sanctions lists); hour commitments; code samples or contributions. |
| Development banks / international orgs | Member-country citizenship; separate calls per term. |
